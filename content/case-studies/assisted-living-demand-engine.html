Case study · Assisted living & memory care · Tennessee

A demand engine for a 52-unit community under new ownership.

New owners, a building being renovated top to bottom, a 25-year reputation to protect and a census to rebuild — with no marketing department. Over four months we rebuilt how the community finds, answers and converts families, and how leadership sees the numbers.

The situation

A 52-unit assisted living and memory care community on 3.5 acres, operating since the late 1990s, acquired by new ownership in 2026. Suites, common areas and furnishings were being renovated. The executive director — a licensed administrator with a strong, personal, phone-first way of working referrals — was the entire sales function, and his bandwidth was the ceiling on census.

The website sat with an outside agency at $200 a month, converting little. Referrals from the national placement agencies arrived by email and waited for a human. Google reviews went unanswered. Leadership saw payroll and census when the reports arrived, not when the numbers moved.

What we were asked to do

  • Make every referral get answered in minutes, around the clock, without losing the executive director’s voice or his role as the closer
  • Own the website, make it convert, and stop paying rent on it
  • Put reviews, listings and local search on a routine instead of a to-do list
  • Give ownership the operating numbers daily, in a form that takes two minutes to read

What we built

Referral agent in the ED’s voice

Inbound referral emails from the four placement agencies are parsed and scored (hot / warm / nurture, assisted living or memory care). Within minutes the family gets a call with a warm transfer to the executive director; on no answer, a text and an email in his voice; then a three-call, three-email cadence over four business days. Tours book straight to his calendar; the referral advisor is updated automatically. Pricing and clinical questions route to him for one-tap approval before anything is sent. Quiet hours, STOP handling and de-duplication are built in.

The website, brought in-house

The agency-hosted site was mirrored, rebuilt as a fast static site on the same URLs, and deployed from source on every change. Lead forms submit in the background with an outage fallback and a second delivery path, so a form never dies in a spinner. Search-engine structured data, a sitemap and conversion tracking were wired in from day one.

Reviews and listings on autopilot

A review engine runs three mornings a week: four- and five-star reviews are answered in the community’s voice, lower ratings are drafted for ownership approval, urgent ones are flagged, and a monthly post invites reviews. A weekly Google Business post goes out with a photo. Yelp leads and messages are checked every two hours.

A daily operating brief

Payroll and census pull from the community’s own systems into a short brief in leadership’s inbox every morning — hours against schedule, agency use, census movement, anomalies flagged the day they happen rather than at quarter close.

Content and news engine

A content calendar and approval queue; family-resource articles and senior-care news published to the site on a schedule; headlines from the industry’s publishers refreshed automatically. Everything runs through an approval step so nothing goes live without a human.

Paid search, measured

Search campaigns tied to the form and call conversions on the site, with the same weekly scorecard reviewed by ownership.

What changed

Referrals stopped waiting on one person’s bandwidth.

The executive director still closes every family — but he no longer has to be the one who notices a referral at 9pm. The public record now matches the building: every review answered, the Google rating climbing from 3.6 to 3.8 in a quarter against a 4.0 target, listings corrected and current. The website costs nothing to run and deploys itself. Ownership reads one brief each morning instead of waiting for month-end.

Occupancy figures are shared with references on request.

  • First response to every referral: minutes, 24/7
  • 100% of reviews answered; rating 3.6 → 3.8 in a quarter
  • Website: $200/month agency hosting → $0, owned
  • Daily payroll and census brief to leadership
  • Six automated routines running without staff time
