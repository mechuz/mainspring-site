Case study · Continuing care retirement community · Michigan

Back to budget, off agency, and ready to sell.

A continuing care retirement community whose labor spend had drifted from its budget, with agency staff filling the gaps and department leaders without a daily view of their own productivity. The owner needed the operation stabilized — and needed it to hold through the annual survey and a sale.

The situation

Labor spending and staffing practices were not consistently aligned with approved budgets. Agency staffing had become routine, adding avoidable expense. Department leaders had no daily measure of paid hours per resident day, so variances were discovered at month-end, not on the day they happened.

Staffing models had not kept pace with the local labor market or with residents’ care needs. All of it had to be fixed while preparing for the annual regulatory survey.

What had to happen

  • Bring labor back to budget without thinning care
  • Get off agency staffing for good
  • Give every department leader a daily number they own
  • Hold the gains through the annual survey
  • Hand ownership a stable operation it could sell

What was done

Agency eliminated

Reliance on agency staffing removed and internal staffing controls strengthened, shift by shift.

Budget-based staffing

Staffing models recalibrated to the approved budget, residents’ needs and the local workforce.

Daily PPD management

Department leaders trained to read paid hours per resident day every day and act on variances the same day.

Expense discipline

Unnecessary spending cut and management accountability reinforced.

Survey readiness

Staff and operations prepared for the annual inspection as the changes went in, not after.

What changed

Savings that showed up every month, and held.

Roughly $40,000 a month in labor savings was sustained for eight months — $320,000 in all. At that rate the annualized equivalent is about $480,000; that is a projection, not additional realized savings.

The community completed its annual regulatory survey (an 8-point result under CMS scoring). With the operation stable and the numbers credible, ownership completed its sale.

  • Agency staffing: eliminated
  • Staffing model aligned to budget and market
  • Department heads managing daily PPD
  • Annual survey completed during the turnaround
  • Sale supported; price impact not quantified
