Mainspring Healthcare Partners · Capabilities for CDPHEWhere we are engaged

For the Department, the Attorney General and the courts

Every remedy Colorado can impose on a failing facility needs someone qualified to run the building while it is imposed.

Colorado counts a receiver or temporary manager, a directed plan of correction, and intermediate conditions such as a required consultant or Department monitoring among its enforcement remedies. We are built to be that temporary manager, receiver, consultant or monitor — and operator of record when an order calls for one — for skilled nursing and nursing care facilities, assisted living residences including secured memory care, and continuing care communities.

Where we are engaged

  • Temporary management imposed as an enforcement remedy, state or federal, where the Department needs a qualified administrator in the building immediately and reporting it can rely on
  • Receivership under C.R.S. 25-3-108 — court-appointed operation of a long-term care facility through to lease, sale or orderly closure, led by a licensed nursing home administrator as the statute requires
  • Independent monitoring and consultation under a directed plan of correction, conditional licence, settlement or consent order, with verification the Department does not have to take on faith
  • Managed closure and resident relocation when a licence is surrendered, suspended or revoked — safe transfers, family communication, records and resident trust funds accounted for
  • Rapid stabilization after immediate-jeopardy findings or an abandonment event, where the priority is resident safety tonight
  • Forensic accounting of a facility's funds, payroll and related-party transactions for the Department, the receiver or the Attorney General

What the Department gets

  • A licensed administrator and a director of nursing in the building within days, matched to Colorado licensure and the facility's survey history
  • A first-72-hour resident-safety assessment and a written stabilization plan
  • Weekly reporting in the format the Department or the court specifies: census, staffing, clinical risk, cash, vendor status, open deficiencies
  • Financial control from day one — cash, payroll, resident trust funds, accounts payable — with an independent accounting at the end
  • A defined exit: transfer to a qualified operator, a sale, or an orderly closure with every resident placed
  • Fixed, pre-agreed fees that fit a state contract, a court order or an enforcement remedy

How this fits Colorado's framework

Enforcement activity under C.R.S. 25-1.5-103 includes civil money penalties, conditional licensure, suspension or revocation, appointment of a receiver or temporary manager, a directed plan of correction, and intermediate conditions such as a required consultant, Department monitoring or additional training. Receivership under C.R.S. 25-3-108: when a licence is revoked, not renewed or summarily suspended, the Department, licensee or owner may ask the district court to appoint a receiver — who must be a licensed nursing home administrator, operates under Department standards, and serves up to 180 days unless extended — so residents are not moved before a lease, sale or closure is arranged. Federal temporary management under 42 C.F.R. 488.415 remains available for certified facilities as an alternative to termination.

Summarized for orientation only; the governing text is the current Colorado Revised Statutes, 6 CCR 1011-1 and 42 C.F.R. Part 488. We work within the specific order, contract or remedy that governs each engagement.
