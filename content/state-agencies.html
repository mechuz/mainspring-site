Where we are engaged

  • Temporary management imposed as an enforcement remedy, where the agency needs a qualified administrator in the building immediately and reporting it can rely on
  • Receivership and court-appointed operations for a facility in financial or operational collapse, run through to transfer, sale or orderly closure
  • Independent monitoring under a settlement, consent order or corporate integrity obligation, with verification the agency does not have to take on faith
  • Managed closure and resident relocation when a licence is surrendered or revoked — safe transfers, family communication, records and trust funds accounted for
  • Rapid stabilization after immediate-jeopardy findings or an abandonment event, where the state’s priority is resident safety tonight
  • Forensic accounting of a facility’s funds, payroll and related-party transactions for the agency, the receiver or the attorney general

What the agency gets

  • A licensed administrator and a director of nursing in the building within days, matched to the state’s licensure and survey history
  • A first-72-hour resident safety assessment and a written stabilization plan
  • Weekly reporting in the format the agency or the court specifies: census, staffing, clinical risk, cash, vendor status, open deficiencies
  • Financial control from day one — cash, payroll, resident trust funds, AP — with an independent accounting at the end
  • A defined exit: transfer to a qualified operator, a sale, or an orderly closure with every resident placed
  • Fixed, pre-agreed fees that fit a state contract, a court order or an enforcement remedy

Our principals and network are working administrators and nurse leaders — people who hold the licence, have run buildings through survey, and have taken over failing operations before. That is the difference between a report that describes the problem and a building that is safe tonight.

We run our own operating businesses on the latest AI computing, and every mandated engagement gets the same stack: daily census, staffing and cash briefs, payroll anomaly monitoring, and a documentary record the agency can audit.
